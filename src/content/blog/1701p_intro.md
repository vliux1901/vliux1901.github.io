## A Brief Introduction

The Crosman 1701P Silhouette is a PCP air pistol built for silhouette competition. Its **quiet report, accuracy with my preferred setup, and roughly 32 consistent shots per fill** make it enjoyable to shoot. The simple shape appeals to me, too. Loading can be fiddly, though, and the grips leave room for improvement—details that matter once I start shooting.

## Appearance and Build

The slim barrel, exposed reservoir, and angular breech give the 1701P a clean, purposeful look. Fine machining lines and a silver-colored trigger add detail, while my wooden grips bring some warmth to the dark metalwork.

![Side profile of the Crosman 1701P showing the exposed barrel, air reservoir, wooden grip, and Williams rear sight](../../assets/1701p/1701p_hero_3.jpg)

## Key Features and My Setup

Crosman describes the 1701P as a **.177, single-shot, bolt-action PCP pistol** with a reversible bolt. Its published length is 14.75 inches and factory weight is 2.8 lb; I have not weighed my equipped pistol.

The **Lothar Walther barrel is a key factory feature for accuracy**. [Crosman describes the German-made precision barrel as providing consistent accuracy](https://www.crosman.com/product/crosman-1701p-silhouette-competition-pcp-air-pistol-177-caliber/)—a useful strength when aiming at small silhouette targets. My measured target groups are still to come.

I made two changes to the factory setup:

- **[Wooden grips](#grip-ergonomics-and-balance):** I replaced the original black plastic panels with the checkered wooden grips shown in my photos.
- **[Williams rear sight](#williams-rear-sight):** The pistol comes without a rear sight. I added the Williams adjustable sight to keep the setup lightweight for open-sight silhouette competition.

My photographed setup also has the bolt handle on the left side.

### Filling and the Pressure Gauge

The fill connection sits beneath a removable cap at the front of the reservoir. **I like how easily it connects to my air pump:** the female quick-disconnect (QD) connector on my pump hose fits directly onto the pistol's fill port, with no extra adapter needed.

I also like the downward-facing pressure gauge: I can read it from underneath the pistol without putting my face in front of the muzzle. It is a thoughtful design detail.

The [owner's manual](https://support.crosman.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201669374/1701P-515.pdf) specifies a **3,000 psi maximum fill pressure** and a Foster #12FS or #12FSS quick-disconnect connection. The appropriate working fill depends on the pistol's tune. The gauge photograph is a separate detail view, not a record of the chronograph session's starting pressure.

With the pistol's relatively small reservoir, **fill slowly when using a large air tank** and watch the pressure closely. Crosman's manual also instructs tank users to open the valve slowly: rapid filling generates heat that can affect the pressure reading.

In my tests, starting fills of roughly **2,250–2,500 psi** have worked well with JSB Exact 8.44-grain pellets, giving about **32–34 consistent shots around 445–455 fps**. These are my results with this pistol and setup.

## Grip, Ergonomics, and Balance

The wooden grips I fitted have checkered panels and a pronounced ledge near the top. The photograph below shows them on the pistol beside the original black plastic grip panels supplied from the factory.

![My added wooden grips fitted to the 1701P beside the original factory black plastic grip panels](../../assets/1701p/1701p_grips.jpg)

**The factory plastic grips feel cheap to me**. I can also feel a little **grip movement when cocking**, which detracts from the otherwise simple, purposeful design. The grip panels use a single screw each; I would prefer a more secure feel.

The grip feels comfortable in my hand, but it is a little thin and offers limited support at the base of my hand. **For one-handed shooting, I would like more support.** For silhouette matches, I would therefore prefer to use a two-handed hold.

**The pistol feels very well balanced.** Most of the weight feels concentrated near the grip, with only a barely noticeable forward bias.

## Trigger and Sights

### Trigger

Crosman specifies a **two-stage adjustable drop-sear trigger**. My close-up shows the curved blade and the adjustment hardware around the trigger guard. [Crosman trigger description](https://www.crosman.com/product/crosman-1701p-silhouette-competition-pcp-air-pistol-177-caliber/).

![Curved trigger blade and adjustment hardware on the Crosman 1701P](../../assets/1701p/1701p_trigger.jpg)

At the factory setting, the **pull feels quite light to me**. The **first stage is short**, followed by a clear wall marking the second stage. A little more pull fires the pistol, with **very little second-stage travel**, similar to my [HW45](/blog/hw45_intro/) and [HW75](/blog/hw75_intro/). Although the wall is easy to feel, the release is not quite as crisp as either Weihrauch's trigger to me. But overall it's a good trigger that contributes to the accuracy.

The **overtravel screw in the trigger guard** acts as an adjustable stop, limiting how far the trigger continues moving after the shot releases. It lets the shooter reduce excess movement after the break for a more controlled follow-through. Crosman explains its purpose in [section 9E of the owner's manual](https://support.crosman.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201669374/1701P-515.pdf#page=7).

### Williams Rear Sight

The 1701P **does not come with a rear sight from the factory**, as noted in the [Crosman manual](https://support.crosman.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201669374/1701P-515.pdf). I chose the [Crosman Williams rear sight](https://www.pyramydair.com/product/crosman-williams-rear-sight-notched-blade-elevation-windage-adj-3-8?a=2008) because I want to keep the pistol lightweight and plan to enter silhouette competitions using open sights.

The Williams sight on mine has a plain rear notch, separate knurled adjustment knobs, and visible reference scales for elevation and windage.

### Rotating Front Sight

The front blade can rotate to change its apparent width in the rear notch. The side set screw secures that adjustment, as described in the [manual's sight section](https://support.crosman.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201669374/1701P-515.pdf).

I investigated the three small recesses on the front-sight housing by shining a flashlight into the muzzle opening. I could not see any light coming through them. Together with their shallow appearance in the photograph, this suggests they are **dimples rather than gas ports**, although the flashlight test alone is not conclusive. Their intended purpose remains unconfirmed.

## Loading and Shooting Experience

The open breech exposes the loading trough and pellet probe. **Loading needs attention:** if the pellet is not properly aligned with the barrel entrance, it sometimes catches in the breech instead of feeding smoothly.

The photo below shows a loading failure I have encountered. When this happens, I have to guide the pellet head into the barrel entrance by hand, then push the bolt forward to seat the rest of the pellet. It is a small but recurring inconvenience that makes careful pellet alignment important.

![Misaligned pellet caught at the barrel entrance in the Crosman 1701P breech](../../assets/1701p/1701p_loading_failure.jpg)

The manual crossbolt safety sits just behind the trigger. These two photographs show its two positions; the exposed red ring indicates **off safe**, according to the [owner's manual](https://support.crosman.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/201669374/1701P-515.pdf).

The 1701P is **very quiet to my ears**, and I find its noise level friendly to backyard shooting in my setup. That is a personal impression, not a sound-level measurement.

## My Verdict So Far

### What I Like

- **Simple shape.** The clean, uncomplicated profile appeals to me.
- **Downward-facing pressure gauge.** I can read it from underneath the pistol without putting my face in front of the muzzle.
- **Quiet shooting.** Its report is very quiet to my ears and suits my backyard setup.
- **Accuracy with my preferred setup.** I am pleased with how it shoots using JSB Exact 8.44-grain pellets in the pressure range that has worked well for me.
- **Useful shot count.** Roughly 32 consistent shots from one fill gives me plenty of shooting between refills.

### What I Don't Like

- **Pellets sometimes catch during loading.** If a pellet is misaligned, it can get stuck in the breech. I then have to guide the pellet head into the barrel entrance by hand before pushing the bolt forward to seat it fully.
- **Factory grip material.** The plastic panels feel cheap to me.
- **Slight grip movement.** With the single-screw panel mounting, I can feel a little movement when cocking. I would prefer a second screw on each panel for a firmer fit.
- **Coarse pressure-gauge markings.** There are no intermediate marks between 2,000 and 2,500 psi, making it difficult to read the pressure precisely.

So far, the quiet report and useful run of consistent shots are the strongest practical positives for me. Loading and grip security are the main annoyances. I will add more impressions as I spend more time with the pistol, along with measured target results and a fuller account of the trigger and balance.
