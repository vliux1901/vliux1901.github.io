# Airgun Review Template

Use this as a writing outline and test checklist. Keep the main sections consistent, combine short sections when helpful, and omit items that do not apply. An initial-impressions article can leave measurements as **TBD / not tested** and add them later.

The bullets are prompts, not mandatory subheadings. For a short review, prioritize fit and handling, trigger/sights, shooting experience, available test results, and the verdict. Magazine and power-system-specific checks apply only to relevant models.

Write in concise, simple language and preserve the first-person voice. Distinguish personal impressions, your own measurements, and manufacturer claims. Describe the example you tested; do not assume every example behaves identically.

## Section Priorities

Use these priorities to decide how much attention and space each section deserves. Article order does not imply equal importance, and these priorities are not numerical scoring weights.

| Default priority | Sections | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** | 4. Ergonomics and Balance; 5. Trigger and Sights; 6. Shooting Experience | Explain how it feels and how easily I can shoot it well. |
| **Core** | 7. Chronograph Results; 8. Accuracy; 11. My Take So Far | Show measured performance, what I achieved on target, and who the gun suits. |
| **Supporting** | 2. Appearance and Build Quality; 3. Key Features and Design | Explain construction and distinctive functions. |
| **Supporting** | 9. Ownership and Reliability; 10. Comparisons and Value | Help readers judge the longer-term purchase. |
| **Brief context** | 1. Introduction and Review Context; background history and specification summaries | Orient the reader without delaying the review. |

Chronograph and accuracy tests are complementary core sections. Chronograph results establish velocity, energy, and shot-to-shot consistency under the tested conditions; target results show the accuracy I achieved. Neither substitutes for the other. Initial-impressions posts can leave either as TBD / not tested.

Adjust the emphasis to the gun and the stage of the review:

- **Target pistol:** grip fit, balance, trigger, sights, and accuracy lead.
- **Spring pistol:** give extra attention to cocking effort, recoil, hold sensitivity, and practical accuracy.
- **PCP:** give extra attention to filling convenience, velocity consistency across the fill, and usable shot count alongside handling and accuracy.
- **Long-term review:** reliability and maintenance move into the core.
- **Initial impressions:** handling leads; clearly mark unfinished testing as TBD / not tested and keep the verdict provisional.

Give the most space to what matters during shooting and what makes this particular gun different. A distinctive feature, build issue, or ownership problem can deserve core attention regardless of its default category. Consider every section, but combine or shorten sections that add little to the particular review. Evidence, source attribution, and photo privacy requirements apply at every priority level.

## 1. Introduction and Review Context

- What is it, and what kind of shooting is it intended for?
- Why did I choose it, and what did I hope it would offer?
- Exact model, variant, caliber, power system, and relevant configuration.
- Brief model history, predecessor, alternative market names, or family resemblance when useful; distinguish shared appearance from shared mechanics.
- Identify factory equipment versus modifications, replacement grips, or damaged/missing parts on the tested example.
- [Optional] Purchased, borrowed, or supplied for review; disclose sponsorship or affiliate relationships if applicable. Include price paid and purchase date if useful.
- Time owned, approximate shot count, and whether this is an initial or longer-term review.
- [Optional] Condition as received and preparation before testing: new/used, cleaning, adjustments, or repairs. Preserve an as-received baseline when available and distinguish later changes.
- Once testing is complete, preview the one or two findings that best explain the review's overall impression.

## 2. Appearance and Build Quality

- Overall shape, proportions, styling, and visual character.
- Materials, finish, machining, edges, seams, and fit between parts.
- Grip or stock quality; moving-part play and how securely the action closes.
- Weight and dimensions: identify measured values versus published specifications, and bare weight versus the complete shooting setup with sights and accessories.
- Wear, finish changes, or defects observed on this example.
- Exposed or fragile parts and protective features, such as a sight hood; distinguish accidental damage from wear during normal use.

## 3. Key Features and Design

- What distinguishes this model, and what difference does each feature make in use?
- Power system, loading arrangement, capacity, and available power settings.
- Briefly explain how an unusual mechanism works when it helps the reader understand operation or shooting feel. Label interpretations and unconfirmed causes.
- Safety operation and how it fits into the shooting sequence.
- Adjustability: explain what each adjustment changes, using the manual as the source.
- Mounting rails, accessory clearance, and useful compatibility details.
- What comes in the box: case, sights, magazines, single-shot tray, fill adapter, tools, and spare seals where applicable; identify essential extras sold separately.
- A short specifications table where it helps; avoid repeating the same facts throughout.

## 4. Ergonomics and Balance

- Grip shape, angle, texture, palm support, and fit for my hand.
- Tested grip size, my hand size where useful, and suitability for left- or right-handed use; note alternative factory or aftermarket grips and whether I have tried them.
- Trigger reach and access to the safety, loading port, and other controls.
- Specific pressure/support points at the thumb web, middle finger, and trigger-guard undercut, where they affect comfort or stability.
- Where the weight sits: muzzle-heavy, rear-heavy, or centered in the hand.
- How high a pistol sits above the hand; natural pointing and wrist position.
- Comfort and steadiness with one hand, two hands, or a supported hold, as applicable.
- Fatigue during a longer session and whether fit changes with grip technique.
- For rifles: length of pull, cheek weld, shoulder fit, and balance with an optic.

## 5. Trigger and Sights

### Trigger

- First-stage travel, second-stage definition, creep, release, and overtravel.
- Trigger-blade shape, position, and comfort.
- Pull weight if measured: instrument, number of readings, and variation.
- Available adjustments and the setting actually tested.

### Sights and Optics

- Sight picture, contrast, glare, and ease of acquiring a repeatable aim.
- Front-blade width relative to the rear notch, visible light gaps, and plain versus fiber-optic sights; note replaceable blades or inserts where relevant.
- Windage/elevation adjustment and ease of zeroing.
- Adjustment access: tool-free or tools required, readable markings/clicks, and enough adjustment range for the tested distance.
- Optic used, magnification, mount, and any obstruction of loading or cocking.
- Whether the optic focuses/parallax-adjusts at the actual test distance; note eye relief, mounting height, and any observed zero shift or mount movement.

## 6. Shooting Experience

- Describe one complete shooting cycle: cock or fill, load, operate controls, aim, fire.
- Cocking or pumping effort, loading access, pellet seating, and pace of shooting.
- If cocking effort is measured, record peak force and the measurement method/contact point; otherwise describe effort as an impression. Note where in the stroke it becomes demanding.
- For repeaters: magazine loading/insertion, indexing and feeding, last-shot indication, pellet fit, and scope clearance; compare single-loading only if tested.
- Recoil, vibration, muzzle movement, and ease of follow-through.
- Sensitivity to hold, grip pressure, or rest position, if observed.
- Perceived muzzle report and mechanical noise; distinguish these from pellet-trap impact.
- What becomes easier with practice, and what remains inconvenient?

Treat noise descriptions as impressions unless measured under documented conditions. Do not infer loudness from muzzle energy alone.

- Use consistent units and identify whether pellet weight is nominal or measured.
- Retain the full shot string. For a subset, state the exact inclusive shot range and why it was selected.
- State whether statistics were calculated from raw readings or copied from an app. If the app's SD convention is unknown, say so; disclose rounding discrepancies rather than silently changing reported values.
- Discuss first-shot behavior, trends, and consistency without inventing a cause.
- Include a shot-by-shot chart when it reveals a trend. Use comparable axes across setups, or flag differences in scale.
- Compare measured velocity with published claims only with pellet weight and test-condition caveats; a consistent velocity string does not by itself establish accuracy.
- For PCPs: record reservoir capacity, start/end pressure, known regulator/power settings, and usable shot count. Define the velocity band or other criterion used for "usable"; distinguish measured counts from estimates and include the pressure/velocity curve when available.
- For CO₂: record temperature, shot cadence, cartridge condition, and usable shots per cartridge if tested, with the same clear endpoint criterion.
- For multi-pumps: state pump count for each string and compare only manufacturer-permitted settings.

## 8. Accuracy: Pellets and Setups

Record distance, indoor/outdoor conditions, shooting position, support/rest contact point, sights or optic, and shots per group. Describe relevant pellet differences, including head size if known.

- Measure group size center-to-center (C–C) and explain the measurement method.
- Use multiple groups per condition where practical; report typical results as well as the best group.
- Summarize pellet preference: which performed best among those tested, and whether several worked well or results were strongly pellet-dependent. A small selection does not establish the best pellet overall.
- Keep other conditions consistent when comparing pellets or setups. Disclose changes and limited samples.
- Record wind/light, barrel cleaning, and any conditioning shots between pellet types when relevant. Use the same shots-per-group for direct comparisons.
- Separate supported results from unsupported shooting results.
- Include every shot in the primary result. Identify called flyers and explain any separate analysis that excludes them.
- Discuss point-of-impact shifts, repeatability, and practical ease of shooting well.
- Separate group tightness from sight zero: a tight group away from the aiming point can still indicate good precision. Limit conclusions to tested distances; add a second relevant distance when practical.
- Label target photos with pellet, distance, setup, and shot count.
- Describe what I achieved under these conditions; avoid presenting it as the gun's absolute accuracy limit.

## 9. Ownership and Reliability

- Malfunctions, leaks, loose parts, or other issues, with shot-count/time context.
- Routine maintenance required by the manual and ease of accessing service parts.
- For an issue I investigated: describe the symptom, suspected cause, action taken, and observed outcome separately, including unsuccessful fixes and unresolved questions.
- When listing replacement parts, include the manual reference and relevant specifications; distinguish parts actually fitted/tested from untested sourcing candidates.
- Consumables and equipment needed: pellets, cartridges, pump/tank, fill fittings, or mounts.
- Filling or charging convenience, storage, and transport. For PCPs, identify the actual connector/adapter chain, required fill pressure, gauge readability, and practicality with the air source used.
- Quality of the manual, parts diagrams, and service support; ease of obtaining common spares.
- Warranty and repair arrangements if relevant, verified for the seller and region: term, service provider, and who pays shipping.
- Changes since the initial review; label limited experience clearly.

## 10. Comparisons and Value

- Compare with a few relevant alternatives, prioritizing models I have used.
- Explain the practical tradeoffs in fit, handling, trigger, performance, and ownership.
- State when a comparison is based on published information rather than direct experience.
- For numerical comparisons, identify common pellets/settings and any differences in conditions; distinguish separate-session results from a controlled comparison.
- Consider the complete usable setup cost, with dated prices if included.

## 11. My Take So Far

### What I Like

- The strongest positives, supported by the observations or results above.

### What I Would Change

- The main limitations, distinguishing personal fit/preferences from faults.

### Who Is It For?

- Which shooter and use case suit it best?
- How much technique, physical effort, or setup does it demand? Does it work well as supplied, or did my satisfactory results depend on changes?
- Who might prefer a different option, and why?
- Would I buy it again, and what still needs longer-term testing?

Keep the conclusion proportional to the evidence. Chronograph and paper-target results alone do not establish humane hunting suitability or competition compliance.

## Sources, Media, and Related Posts

- Link manuals and sources beside the factual claims they support; date time-sensitive prices and policies.
- Useful photos: overall profile, grip/stock, controls, action/loading area, sights, test setup, chronograph results, and targets.
- Use side-by-side photos for useful comparisons and identify left/right or top/bottom clearly. Add short videos for movement or operation, and diagrams/animations where a still photo cannot explain the mechanism; state the limits of explanatory illustrations.
- Link related reviews, maintenance reports, and mechanism articles. Keep specialist detail in a follow-up when it would overwhelm the main review.
- Before committing or publishing, follow `AGENTS.md`: inspect every added, modified, or newly reused photo at full resolution; precisely mask serial numbers and adjacent machine-readable codes with ImageMagick while preserving other markings.
- Recheck compressed, staged, and generated images and affected page references; keep unredacted originals out of published assets and report photos reviewed/redacted.
